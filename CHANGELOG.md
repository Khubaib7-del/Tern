### Fixed (PR #83): scholarly follow-ups from the #82 Windows test

- **Academic questions search the literature first**: a question about papers, studies, research methods or a named technique ("How does FLARE compare with retrieving before every sentence?", "Do octopuses dream in binary code?") runs `scholar_search` before the model answers, chosen by a deterministic classifier (academic, file, casual or general) rather than left to the model, which picked `web_search` with `scholar_search` on offer. The model is told to answer from the papers, say "not enough evidence" when they do not cover the question, and may still search the web afterwards. The system prompt and tool descriptions say the same, and never to expand an acronym from memory.
- **Snowball ranks by influence and relevance, not date**: `paper_snowball` ranks each round's finds by relevance to the topic, the user's question (new `question` argument) and the seed, then Semantic Scholar's influential citation count and influential links, then citations overall; the year plays no part. The strongest finds are followed in the next round and lead the list (it had listed a seed's newest citing papers, off topic and uncited, first).
- **`paper_read` falls back to the abstract**: when a paper has no open-access PDF, or its site's robots.txt forbids reading it, `paper_read` returns the paper's abstract from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex or Crossref, marked "Abstract only" (and "(abstract only)" in the Ledger), instead of failing. Search and snowball lists say which papers have a PDF and which give their abstract only, and to prefer the readable ones.
- **No claims about unread papers**: every paper list tells the model that none of its papers has been read and that a paper is described only by what its title and abstract say until `paper_read` returns its text ("according to its abstract" for an abstract-only read).
- **A paper's own reference numbers leave quotes**: markers such as [17], [9, 18] or [3–5] in passages and abstracts are taken out before the model sees them, so an answer no longer carries them and the claim check no longer reports "Cites [17] [18] [9] but Tern read no such source".
- **No web search for folder and file tasks**: Tern's own search after a refusal or a repeated reply never runs for a file or folder task (a file name such as `counts.md` or `*.md`, a local path, or files and folders with file tools on offer) or once the model has used a file tool in the turn; "count words in every .md file here and save the counts to counts.md" had been answered "I couldn't find any relevant results".

### Added (PR #83): evidence chain and claim verification

- **Quote ids**: Tern mints a six-digit id for each passage it quotes, hashed from the page and the passage's words, so the same passage always has the same id and an id cannot be guessed. `paper_read` labels each passage with its id and locator (`[q:3f9a1c · p. 4 · 3 Method]`) and lists the quotes in its result.
- **Unforgeable citations**: an answer may cite a passage as `[[q:3f9a1c]]`; the token becomes `[n]` only when Tern minted that id from a page the turn read, and is stripped otherwise. Every `[n]` of a claim is bound to the quote of its source that bears on the claim, or flagged: a page Tern never read, or one with no passage on the claim.
- **Stances and agreement**: every page the turn read (up to 8 per claim, cited ones always) is judged Supports, Refutes, Mixed or Not enough info by the offline checks the claim verdicts use (overlap, numbers, polarity, antonyms, swapped sides and names), so it works with small local models; the chat's model may look again at up to 8 cards in one call. Support and refute are counted per independent site into each claim's agreement, with "few sources" under three.
- **Counter-search**: the headline claim and claims with a number or a strong word (never a hedged one) get one search for evidence against them, two claims per answer and two new pages each, when claims are judged by the model; the pages join the agreement and the run shows **Counter-searching** and **Counter-searched** steps.
- **Report › Review claims**: each claim shows its stance chip, an agreement bar (solid support, mid-grey mixed, outlined refute), one card per source with its quote, page or section, logo and stance (counter-search cards marked), citations Tern could not back, and the counter-search line; a line above the claims sums the stances. **Citation chips** show the quote card on hover. The run report gains an Evidence section (Markdown) and an `evidence` field (JSON).
- **Evaluation**: an `evidence@1` suite (10 cases) with citation validity, verdict accuracy, agreement accuracy, counter-search trigger accuracy and counter-search effect accuracy. Docs: [docs/evidence.md](docs/evidence.md).
- **Docs**: the README covers everything through #83, and the roadmap lists shipped pull requests #71–#82 and the plan for #84–#103.

### Added (PR #82): scholarly search

- **Scholarly tools** (with web search on): `scholar_search` asks Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, arXiv and Crossref at once (CORE too with a key), reads every answer into one paper shape (title, authors, year, venue, DOI, arXiv id, citations, abstract, a legal open-access PDF) and merges a paper several services found into one; `paper_read` reads a paper's open-access PDF (arXiv, the services' links, or Unpaywall once your email is saved) through Tern Crawl and quotes passages with page and section locators ("p. 4 · 3 Method"); `paper_snowball` follows references and citations round by round until new relevant papers level off, a round's yield falls too low, or a cap is reached.
- **Rerank funnel and "not enough evidence"**: relevance (BM25 over title and abstract or passage text, plus coverage of the question's key terms) blended 80/20 with quality (citations, recency, an open-access copy); retracted papers (Crossref notices, OpenAlex's flag) never pass. When nothing passes the gate, the tool tells the model there is not enough evidence in the literature searched and to say so rather than answer from memory; `paper_read` does the same for a paper with no supporting passage.
- **Scholarly HTTP** (`tern_crawl::ScholarHttp`): each service's own pace (arXiv one request every 3 s, Semantic Scholar 1 s, CORE 6 s), 429 and 5xx retried with backoff (`Retry-After` honoured up to 30 s), answers cached a day in memory and under `<data>/cache/scholar`; one failing service is named and the others' papers still ranked.
- **Papers in the Sources ledger and as sources**: the run panel's **Ledger** shows each paper with its authors, year, venue, citation count and open-access PDF link; papers read are the answer's sources, with the service's logo (new logos for OpenAlex, CORE, Crossref and Unpaywall).
- **Settings › Search & Crawlers › Scholarly sources**: one row per service with its logo, what it gives and what it needs; **Add key** / **Add email** (with **Get a key**), **Test** (searches one paper with what is saved) and **Remove**; keys and emails go to the OS keychain as `scholar.<id>`. Only CORE needs a key.
- **Evaluation**: a `scholarly@1` suite (6 cases over answers recorded in each service's format) with evidence accuracy, recall at k, dedupe, retracted-kept-out, locator, claim-support and snowball-stop metrics, in the CI baseline.
- **UI tour**: `TERN_TOUR_ONLY=scholarly` plays an academic question answered from recorded answers (papers searched and read, quotes with locators), its inline sources, the Ledger's paper rows, and the Scholarly sources card.

### Fixed (PR #82): follow-ups from the #80 Windows test

A model that writes it again after being told ends the turn answered ("counts.md is already written. The model kept writing it again …"), not failed. The counts are kept in the checkpoint, so **Continue** does not start them over (codestral wrote counts.md nine times in one turn, then four more after Continue).

### Fixed (PR #80): Act mode guards from the #79 Windows test

- **Folder chats start in the folder's mode, not Chat**: a new chat on a folder starts in the mode last used in that folder (Chat, Plan or Act, saved per folder path when you pick a mode or send), and in **Act** for a folder that has none. A reopened folder chat with no mode of its own takes its folder's.
- **"This needs Act mode" instead of a failed tool**: in Chat mode a folder chat reads but does not change files. A request that plainly asks to make or change a file ("write a review of sum.md to review.md", "rename draft.md to hello.md") is answered before any model call; a write the model calls anyway, a task graph that plans one, a script that calls one, or an answer that hands the work back ("Please manually create a file named reviews.md and paste the content", "Would you like me to execute it?") ends the same way. The answer says what it needs and a card over the composer offers **Switch to Act and run**, which switches the chat and its folder to Act and sends the message again. The Chat-mode prompt tells the model it has no write tools and never to plan them or tell the user to create files by hand (`mode.needs_act` event).
- **Code mode: a script cannot define the tool functions**: a `run_python` script that defines or assigns `file_write`, `file_rename` or another provided function (ministral wrote `def file_write(...): return "File written successfully"`) is not run; the model is told the functions already exist and do the real work. A code-mode script that called no tool while the user asked for a file action gets one line saying nothing was read, written or renamed.
- **Code mode: a failed call raises**: a refused or failed call inside a script raises `ToolError` with its reason (uncaught, the `run_python` card shows failed and the script's later "saved" print never runs). An answer that says a file was saved, written or created while that file's last write failed (and none succeeded after) is sent back once: "counts.md was not written: …" (`model.false_claim`).
- **Code mode: real Python values**: `files_list()` returns a list of `{"path", "size", "is_dir"}` dicts and `file_read()` the file's text, as the stubs in the prompt now say. pathlib's `read_text`, `write_text` and `iterdir` in a script go through `file_read`, `file_write` and `files_list` (each still approved), so such a script runs in code mode instead of failing with "OS function not implemented".
- **Requests over a provider's token cap**: a 413 "Request too large … Limit 8000, Requested 17096" (Groq's per-minute cap) is sent again once, made to fit: the earlier chat cut to its last exchange, long messages to their head and tail, the tool list to one line per tool and only the tools the turn needs, the reply's room to what is left. If it still cannot fit, the turn says "gpt-oss-120b on your Groq plan accepts 8000 tokens per request; this chat needs about N. Pick a model with a larger limit or start a new chat." with no raw JSON. A failed quick turn's banner says **Retry**, not "Retry failed workers".
- **The chat model picker lists chat models**: guard, safeguard, prompt-guard, voice, OCR and embedding models leave the composer's model popover (speech and OCR models are still chosen in Settings › Models). A provider listing that marks such a model as chat (Groq's `llama-prompt-guard-2-86m`, `gpt-oss-safeguard-20b`) is overruled by the model's name.
- **Diagrams**: every system prompt says Tern draws ```` ```mermaid ```` blocks (and renders tables and math), so "draw a flowchart" is answered with a diagram instead of "I don't have the tools to draw".
- **Writing requests never search the web**: Tern's own search after a refusal or a repeated reply is skipped for writing and explaining ("write a long essay about arrays", "explain recursion", "draw a flowchart"); asking about something current still searches. A flat refusal of such a request ("I'm sorry, but I can't assist with that request") is asked once more, neutrally. Search snippets that are a site's own marketing ("expertly composed essays … everything you're looking for") are left out of the results the model reads.
- **UI tour**: `TERN_TOUR_ONLY=work` adds x31 (Chat mode in a folder chat: the needs-Act card) and x32 (Switch to Act and run: the same message runs in Act and writes the file).

### Fixed (PR #79): Act mode hardening from the #78 Windows test

- **Outputs only lists files that are there**: an output whose file was deleted, or moved away outside Tern, leaves the title bar's Outputs menu and the Files tab within two seconds (and when a chat is shown or Outputs is opened), and the counts on Outputs and the title bar's file icon count only files that exist. The Outputs header has its own **Refresh** button, like the Folder's; it says how many outputs it removed.
- **A folder named like a path is the folder** (a chat folder called `New`: the model listed `New` and wrote `New/review.md`, and the approval asked for `D:/Codex/New/New/**`). The system prompt names the chat's folder and says paths are relative to it and must not start with its name; a path whose first segment is the folder's own name (and no real subfolder of that name exists) means the folder. `file_write` makes the folders a path needs, inside the chat's folder only; a `mkdir` the command rules refuse tells the model so, and a command whose working folder is gone says that instead of Windows' "The directory name is invalid".
- **No write preview for a refused call**: a `file_write` sent back to the model (empty text, a rewrite of a file you asked to add to) no longer opens a "Writing… +0 −0" preview in the Files tab.
- **Call limits on a plan turn, and Continue on the same model**: in Act mode a turn with an open plan gets twice the model calls and tool calls. When a turn stops at a call limit, its card says which limit and which model, and **Continue** resumes on the same model. (The switch to `ministral-3b` in the test was the model picker used on the paused card; Tern has no automatic fallback.)
- **Wrapped tool calls run**: a call to a tool named `tool` with the real call in its arguments (codestral), or a tool name that is itself a JSON call (ministral, stray braces included), runs as the call inside.
- **Per-minute rate limits are retried**: a 429 that says "tokens per minute" or "requests per minute" and "try again in 41.87s" waits the stated time (at most 60 s) and retries; only a real quota or billing error stops the turn.
- **A printed script is run, not shown**: in Act mode with a folder, when the user asked for a script that does something and the model answers with a Python block and "how to use this script", it is told once to run it with `run_python` and the tool functions (`file_write(path, text)`, …). A Python answer to a plain question is left alone.
- **"Add a line" keeps the file**: a `file_write` that rewrites a file the user asked to add to and drops existing lines goes back to the model ("Call `file_edit` on sum.md …") without a card; a whole-file write that only adds runs. Every write's card shows **+N −M**, also when a rule or "for this chat" allowed it with no card.
- **The Files tab's preview follows a rename**: after a script writes `notes.md` and renames it to `hello.md`, the preview is `hello.md` · Saved, not `notes.md` · Not written; the preview and its diff read the file at the folder-relative path.
- **`run_python` in a task graph**: the refusal tells the model to call `run_python` separately.
- **The Interrupted note wraps** inside the chat column instead of running past the composer.
- **Mermaid log**: a diagram drawn after its labels were quoted logs one line; merman's error is logged only when quoting did not help.
- **UI tour**: `TERN_TOUR_ONLY=work` adds x29 (code mode writes then renames: the preview follows the new name) and x30 (a file deleted outside Tern leaves Outputs and the counts drop).

### Fixed (PR #78): planner and code mode fixes from the #77 Windows test

- **A plan the model stops updating no longer freezes** (the plan card sat at "0 of 3 done" for a whole run with `ministral-14b-2512`). After every round of tools that did not update the plan, the model reads one line saying where the plan stands and to call `plan` when a step is finished; the `plan` tool's description says the same. When the run ends with steps still open, Tern logs the plan once more as **ended**: the card reads "Plan · ended at 1 of 3 done" and the open steps say **Not marked done** (shown as skipped, never as done: Tern does not guess which steps the model finished). Chats logged before this show the same once their run has ended.
- **Task ids in the graph card stay on one line**: a long id (`read_sum`) was wrapped a letter at a time in a 22 px column; ids now take their width up to 96 px and end in an ellipsis.
- **Code mode says plainly there is no disk in Python**: the system prompt and `run_python`'s description say that `open()`, `pathlib` and `os` do not work and that files and the web are reached only through the tool functions, with a two- or three-line example built from the functions this chat has (`names = files_list()`, `text = file_read("notes.md")`, …). A script that still reaches for them (`NotImplementedError: OS function 'Path.iterdir' not implemented`, `No module named 'os'`, `name 'open' is not defined`) gets the error back with a note naming the functions to use instead.
- **No empty files, no card for nothing**: a `file_write` with empty text that would create a file (or fill an empty one) goes straight back to the model asking for the content; no approval card is shown. In a task graph, a `{{id}}` whose task has no result yet or an empty one, or a `{{id.field}}`, fails that task instead of running it with the placeholder or an empty text.
- **`file_edit`** (new, Act mode): adds lines at the end (`append`) or replaces one exact passage that is in the file once (`find` / `replace`; Windows line endings matched). It runs as the `file_write` of the whole new text, so it shows the same approval card with a small diff ("+1 −0" for an added line), follows the same permission rules and Settings › Tools entry of its own, and empties the read cache like a write. A passage that is not there, or is there twice, comes back to the model with what to do.
- **No prose "Shall I?" before a change**: the tool rules say the approval card is the confirmation and to call the tool directly. When the user asked for a change and the model still answers "Would you like me to proceed with adding this line?", Tern tells it once to call the tool (`model.asked_in_prose`); a question that is not about a change the user asked for is left alone.
- **"read sum.md" reads the file**: a message that plainly asks to read, show or open a file in the chat's folder ("read sum.md", "what's in review.md?") runs `file_read` before the model answers, so the answer is the file as it is now and not the model's memory of what it wrote; the tool rules also say to call `file_read` for such a request every time.
- **UI tour**: `TERN_TOUR_ONLY=work` adds x26–x28 (a plan left open ends where it stopped; "add a line" as a `file_edit` card with +1 −0, then approved), and x22's graph uses long task ids.

### Added (PR #77): planner, task graph, code mode, tool result cache

- **Plan tool for normal chats** (`tern-agent::planner`, `plan`): for a task with several steps the model shows a short plan and updates it as it goes (each step pending, in progress or done; one in progress). Each update is a `plan.updated` event, and the chat shows a compact **plan card** above the answer's tool cards that changes live (grey marks, the cards' done green, a thin progress bar, "2 of 4 done"; click to fold to the step in progress). `plan` calls have no tool card of their own. The TUI prints the plan as one line each time it changes ("Plan · 1 of 3 done: ✓ … · ▸ … · ○ …"). Plans are read leniently: steps as plain strings, statuses in any common spelling ("In-Progress", "doing", "completed"), a JSON string inside a code fence.
- **Task graph** (`run_tasks`): the model hands Tern a JSON DAG of tool calls (`{"tasks": [{"id", "tool", "args", "after"}]}`); tasks that do not wait for each other run in parallel (waves; reads, searches and pure work three at a time, changes one by one after them), and `{{id}}` in a task's arguments is replaced by that task's result (a lone `{{id}}` in a URL field takes the first link). Every task goes through the policy gate on its own, so a write in a graph still shows its approval card. Small local models are helped: `depends_on`/`deps`/`needs` for `after`, numbers for ids, args as a JSON string, a whole graph in a fence, duplicate ids renamed, unknown or self dependencies dropped (and said); a cycle, an unknown tool or more than 12 tasks comes back to the model as one line with an example. The plan card shows the graph with each task's id, tool, what it waits for and its status; the calls nest under the `run_tasks` card. A task whose dependency failed is skipped.
- **Code mode** (`tern-agent::python`): inside `run_python` the chat's tools are Python functions with typed stubs listed in the system prompt (`def web_search(query: str) -> str`), so one script can search, read and compare many things and only what it prints comes back to the model. Monty resolves a stub's name to a host function; each call goes from the worker to Tern on stdout and its answer comes back on stdin (`sandbox::run_hosted`), and Tern runs it as a call of the turn, through the same permission and approval engine (in Ask mode a write inside a script shows its card; declining it stops the turn). At most 40 calls per script; the interpreter's 20 s limit excludes the waits. The script's card shows its calls nested under it, folded into one row ("4 tool calls inside: file_read ×3, file_write") that opens by itself when one waits for you or failed. A program that calls no tool runs as before.
- **Tool result cache** (`tern-agent::toolcache`): a search, page fetch or file read asked again with the same arguments (key order, spacing and a URL's fragment ignored) gets its earlier result: web search 1 h, scholarly search 1 day, `read_url` 6 h, kept per chat in memory and on disk under the data folder (`cache/tools`) so another chat gets it too; file and vault reads 10 min, in memory only, and emptied by any write, rename or command. Writes, renames and commands are never cached, nor are failures. The card of a cached result says **cached** (hover: how old), and a folded step row says so too. `TERN_NO_TOOL_CACHE=1` turns it off.
- **UI tour**: `TERN_TOUR_ONLY=work` adds x21–x25: the plan card, a parallel task graph, code mode with its calls nested and the write inside waiting for its card, the script done and folded, and a cache hit.

### Fixed (PR #76)

### Changed (PR #67): search quality and GPU speech

From the Windows `tern.log` of #66: every search went to the keyless Wikipedia; `harness2 by Google AI model or project` found nothing after 5 tries and `SubQ Snap 1.1 AI model definition or meaning` came back with six off-topic pages (Japanese, medical); Whisper small took 19–30 s for an 11.7 s Urdu clip on a 4-core, 8-thread laptop with 6 threads.

- **Off-topic search results are dropped** (`relevance.rs`): a result must share one of the question's main terms (quoted phrases, words with digits like `harness2`, a name with its version like `Snap 1.1`, inner-capital names like `SubQ`; else capitalised names; else keywords without generic words). A generic word (`model`, `project`, `definition`, `AI`…) alone never lets a page through and is never a fallback query on its own; a version number alone is never a term. `tern.log` records `found=… filtered=… results=… terms=[…]` for each search.

- **Short keyword queries**: the `web_search` tool asks the model for 2–6 keywords (names, products, versions) and one call per topic; the keyword fallback ladder stays.
- **Which service answered**: the status line says "Searching Wikipedia…" (or Tavily, Brave, SearXNG), and the answer's rollup "Searched 1 on Wikipedia · 6 off-topic dropped" (a chat turn's search was not counted at all before: the rollup counted only deep workers' `tool.started` steps).
- **Whisper on the GPU**: whisper.cpp's Vulkan backend on Windows and Linux and Metal on macOS, found at run time with automatic fallback to the CPU (no device, no driver, a model the GPU cannot load, or a GPU run that took the app down last time). `vulkan-1.dll` is delay-loaded with a failure hook, so Tern starts and transcribes on the CPU on a PC without a Vulkan driver. Every transcription logs its device (`engine=whisper.cpp gpu=vulkan <device>` or `cpu N threads (why)`). See [docs/speech.md](docs/speech.md).
- **Settings › Speech**: Device (Auto/GPU/CPU) and CPU threads (Auto/2/4/6/8), saved in `preferences.json` (`speech_device`, `speech_threads`); the saved threads win over `TERN_WHISPER_THREADS`. **Auto threads are capped at 4** (was 6): 4 was fastest on the 4-vCPU runners and 6 was still slow on the laptop.
- **CI**: the Vulkan SDK is installed (cached) on Windows and Linux; on Windows without the runtime, on purpose. The desktop job checks that `vulkan-1.dll` is a delayed import of `tern-desktop.exe` and `tern.exe` and that `tern-desktop.exe` keeps running on the runner; the speech benchmark adds device rows (Auto, GPU asked for, and on Linux lavapipe standing in for a GPU).
- **Urdu typed in the composer ends on the left on Windows**: an ASCII `?`, `.` or `!` typed at the end of an Urdu line (an English keyboard layout) sat on the right, the sentence's start, because DirectWrite laid every line out as a left-to-right paragraph. `third_party/gpui-pre-windows` now lays out a line whose first strong letter is right-to-left as a right-to-left paragraph (`dir=auto`, as cosmic-text already does on Linux). Still open: the composer's Urdu lines are left-aligned and its caret and selection follow gpui-component's left-to-right input (a later PR).
- **Tests**: relevance fixtures shaped like the user's log (off-topic SubQ and harness pages, a mixed page set), fallback-ladder cases, speech thread/device rules, rollup and status wording; UI tour scenes c17 (off-topic Wikipedia results and the notice), c18–c19 (Settings › Speech and the GPU probe), c20 (Urdu typed in the composer) and t59c.

### Fixed (PR #57)

Explain in 3D's Next, Play, zoom, Reset and hover no longer close the app (Windows). The cause is now known from the crash log, and it was not the one #55 and #56 inferred.

- **The cause**: `crash.log` (from #56's watchdog) shows `panic at gpui-pre-0.3.9/src/window.rs:5353: called Option::unwrap() on a None value`, then "panic in a function that cannot unwind", exit `0xC0000409`, with `CallWindowProcW` on the stack and "Explain in 3D: step 1 of 16" as the last action. Line 5353 is `Window::current_view()`, which unwraps the top of GPUI's rendered-view stack. `Window::request_animation_frame()` calls it, and the stack holds a view only while GPUI renders, prepaints or paints one. Tern asked for the next frame with `request_animation_frame()` inside click, mouse, wheel and pinch listeners, where the stack is empty, so it panicked; on Windows a listener runs inside the window procedure, an `extern` function that cannot unwind, so the process aborted. Linux and macOS would have panicked too, on the same clicks.
- **Corrected**: #56's "stack overflow on the 1 MB Windows main thread" and #55's "label layout and batching" were inferences, made without a Windows crash report. Neither was the cause of the closing. The 8 MB main-thread stack, the path and label budgets and the label layout work stay: they are sound on their own, just not the fix for this crash.
- **Now**: Tern asks for frames with `view::frame::next_frame(window, cx)`, which notifies its own view (`cx.entity_id()`) once the next frame is drawn, and `next_frame_for(window, id)` in the paint callbacks and popover content that have no `Context`. Both are correct in a listener, a timer, an action, a task or a render. Every call was changed, also the ones that ran in a render and were safe, so the habit cannot come back: Explain in 3D's step click, pointer move (hover), press, release, wheel zoom, pinch, the stage-size and error repaints in its canvas, the level and step dots, Reset, Play and Next; the timeline rail's click glide (it also crashed); the workspace's render-time requests (flashcard turn, theme wipe and sliding panels, highlight and knobs), the toast, Scout and Explain ticks, the context ring's sweep, the run map's figure, and the inner-list wheel listener (`wheel_first`, which read `current_view()` in a paint callback and now takes the view's id).
- **Guard**: `dependency_rules` fails if `request_animation_frame` or `current_view(` appears anywhere in a crate's source (outside the tests in `view/frame.rs`), and separately if either appears inside a `cx.listener(...)` or `.on_*(...)` body; the scan's own test shows it catches the bad pattern. GPUI test-app tests (`TestAppContext`, GPUI's `test-support` as a dev-dependency) click, wheel and play on a stand-in stage and deliver frames with `simulate_next_frame`, and show that `request_animation_frame` outside a render panics.
- Audited, no change needed: GPUI's own elements read `current_view` while they lay out, prepaint and paint (`div`, `img`, `list`, `text`, `with_animation`, `use_asset`, `use_keyed_state`, `defer_draw`), and Tern builds and paints elements only from renders and canvas callbacks, never from a listener. The crash watchdog stays.

### Added (PR #36)

- **Study materials** (`tern-study`, GPUI-free and tested offline; `tern_agent::study`): a Study session is grounded in the user's own materials: files, folders, and `.zip`s through the attachment reader (PDFs page by page with their `[page N]` markers), vault notes, a chat's latest research report, and web pages or online PDFs read through Tern Crawl. Without a model, or when its reply cannot be read, topics come from the materials' headings, or from runs of chunks named by their key words, with objectives from the definitions they contain.
- **Mastery**, simple and explainable: per topic an Elo-style rating from 0 to 1 (a graded answer moves it by how much it beat what the rating expected at that difficulty) and a Leitner box from 1 to 5 (right moves up, wrong back to 1, partly right stays), which sets when the topic is next due (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days). - **Quizzes**: typed, serialisable `Quiz` with multiple choice, short answer, true or false, and fill in the blank questions, each with its answer key, explanation, and source anchor; count (1 to 30), difficulty (easy, medium, hard), and types are configurable. The model writes them from the passages; its reply is read leniently (`B`, `b)`, or the choice text as the answer, `true`/`T`, loose field names) and any question that cannot be checked is dropped. Without a model, a deterministic writer builds questions from the passages' sentences (blanks on key terms, real and altered statements, definitions with other terms as distractors). Grading: exact against the key for multiple choice, true or false, and blanks (plurals and one typo in a long word forgiven); short answers by the model judge, with a word-overlap check offline or when the judge cannot be read; each grade has a verdict glyph (✓ ◐ ✕ ○), feedback, and the source page. Results move each question's topic mastery; every quiz and attempt is kept in `quizzes/<id>.json` and the history.
- **Viva practice**: an examiner asks one question at a time on the user's project or materials, levels 1 (recall) to 5 (defend and extend), with one follow-up on a short or off-material answer that points at the passage the answer touched. At the end, a rubric report scores clarity, correctness, depth, and use of sources from 1 to 5 (overall out of 100) with strengths, weaknesses, topics to revise, and a note per question, from the model or, offline, an estimate from answer length, overlap with the materials, and references to them. Saved as `vivas/<id>.json` and `.md`, like run reports.
- Preferences: `study_offline`, `study_quiz_count`, `study_difficulty`, `study_viva_questions`.
- Terminal: `tern study add <files> [--note NAME] [--report CHAT] [--link URL]`, `tern study list|rm|topics [--rebuild]|history`, `tern tutor [topic] [--mode explain|socratic|new]`, `tern quiz [topic] [--n N] [--difficulty D] [--kinds mcq,short,tf,blank] [--json]`, `tern viva [--title T] [--n N]`; each takes `--offline`.
- Desktop: the **Study** harness pill opens the Study screen on a warmer surface with round markers, pill radius, and italic serif labels: a Study home (materials with their icons or site logos, topics with mastery rings, due markers, and their first objective, the Tutor, Quiz, and Viva actions, history, and Study settings for quiz length, difficulty, viva length, and using the model), the tutor chat with mode chips and cited passages under each reply, the quiz card (one question at a time, instant feedback with the verdict glyph, the answer revealed with its page, a score summary with a ring), and the viva screen with a running timer, per-question time, and the rubric report.

### Added (PR #35)

- **Run checkpoints and resume** (`tern_agent::checkpoint`, GPUI-free): a research run saves its state to `<data>/checkpoints/<chat>.json` (versioned JSON, written atomically, one per chat, removed when the run answers). Quick turns save at the start and after every tool result; deep research saves when the plan is approved, after each worker's reads, after each worker's notes, when a worker ends, and before the answer, keeping each read page's text cut to what a worker is shown. `ChatSession::resume_run` picks a stopped, crashed, or force-quit run up: a quick turn goes on with its conversation so far; a deep run keeps its approved plan (not asked again), finished workers' notes, and unfinished workers' pages and rounds, so no finished worker runs again and no page is read again. Budgets count the tokens used before the stop. These are not the Git screen's checkpoints, which are commits of the vault. Events `run.resumed` and `checkpoint.failed`; turn ends carry `resumable`.
- **Live steering** (`tern_agent::steer`, `tern_research::steer`): a `Steering` inbox per chat; notes sent while a run goes are taken in at the next safe point and logged as `steer.received` and `steer.applied` (with what was done, or why not). A quick turn's model reads the note before its next step. A deep run reads `drop sub-question 3`, `add: cost comparison`, `redirect 2: …`, and anything else as guidance for every notes call still to come and the answer; a dropped worker is stopped with its notes kept, an added one gets a slice like the others (the run's budget grows by it, and the log says so), a running worker takes a redirect as its next search at its next round. A note that arrives while the answer is being written is logged and not applied. Notes from other processes arrive through `<data>/steer/<chat>.txt`.
- **Watcher** (`tern_agent::watch`, `tern_research::watch`): questions on watch live in `<data>/watches.json` with an interval (hourly, daily, weekly, `12h`, `3d`, `2w`). A run is a new chat (a quick turn, or deep research with the plan run as drafted), then the claim checks and report, then a diff against the last report: new and gone sources (matched by URL), changed verdicts (claims matched by their content words), new and dropped claims. No operating-system scheduler: the desktop app runs due watches while it is open, and `tern watch run` from the terminal.
- **Ledgers** (`tern_research::ledger`, `tern_agent::ledger`): from the event log, a source ledger (each URL a search returned or a worker picked: read live, read from the cache, skipped for robots.txt, budget, duplicate, policy, or not picked, or failed, per worker) and a budget ledger (tokens in and out, pages, searches, and time since the lane's previous step, per step, with totals for the run and each worker against its slice). Searches log their hits, and deep workers log the results they leave unread (`sources.skipped`). Both are in the run report (Markdown and JSON, `ledger`), with the steering notes and where a resumed run picked up.
- Terminal: `tern resume [chat] [--list] [--discard] [--json]`, `tern steer <chat> <note>`, `tern watch add|list|rm|run`, `tern ledger [chat] [--turn N] [--json]`; `tern research --deep` takes steering notes typed while it runs and prints how to resume it.
- Desktop: Enter in the composer while a run goes sends a steering note (a hint line says so; the run panel's Summary lists each note and what it did); a **Resume** banner over the composer for a chat whose run stopped (with Dismiss); a **Ledger** view in the run panel (every URL with its site's logo, fate, and reason, then each lane's tokens, pages, searches, and time against its slice); a **Watches** list in the sidebar and a Watches screen (interval, next run, Run now, Open last run, remove, and what changed with source logos and verdict glyphs); an eye under each answer puts its question on watch daily.

### Added (PR #34)

- **Research plans** (`tern_research::plan`, GPUI-free and tested offline): for a deep research request the model drafts a typed, serialisable `ResearchPlan`: 2 to 5 sub-questions (6 at most), each with what to look for, 1 to 3 searches, the expected source types (papers, docs, news, reference, official, data, forums, any), and a share of the budget. Replies are read leniently (fenced or wrapped JSON, a bare list, loose field names, `"40%"` shares); one that is not a plan falls back to Tern's own split (the parts of `A vs B`, a list of questions, or background, evidence, and limits). The run's budget (240k tokens, 24 pages, 600 s by default) is split by share with largest remainder, floors of 4,000 tokens and one page, and time sized for the workers that run side by side. A preflight token range is shown with the plan.
- **Plan approval**: the plan is put to the user before anything is searched or read: **Approve** (run as drafted), **Edit** (reword or drop sub-questions, then **Run**), **Skip plan** (one worker on the whole question), or **Cancel**. Events `plan.drafted` and `plan.answered` (with `edited`).
- **Parallel workers** (`tern_agent::deep`): each sub-question runs as a worker, up to 3 at once (`deep_workers` in preferences, 1 to 6). A worker searches its planned queries, reads up to 3 new pages per round through the shared Tern Crawl reader (robots.txt, per-host pacing, page cache), and writes short notes citing its pages; it may ask for one more search. Each keeps to its own slice of tokens, pages, and time (Over budget and Timed out keep the notes so far), every search and read still goes through the policy gate, and each can be cancelled alone (`DeepControl::cancel_worker`) or with the whole run. Only its notes and their pages reach the synthesis. Events `worker.started` and `worker.finished`; its searches, reads, and model calls are logged as usual with a `worker` field.
- **Synthesis**: the workers' notes are merged into one numbering (the same page read by two workers gets one number), one answer is written from them, its citations are renumbered in order of first use with made-up numbers removed, and a `Sources` list is appended. The claim checks and run report then run as after any turn; the report gains the plan and a **Workers** section (sub-question, status, searches, pages of its slice, tokens of its slice, time, pages read).
- `tern research --deep "question" [--workers N] [--yes] [--json]`: shows the plan and asks to run, edit, skip, or cancel it, prints worker progress, then the answer, the verdict tally, and the report's path.
- Desktop: a **Deep research** toggle in the composer (Research harness), a plan card in the transcript (sub-questions with their searches, source types, and shares, the preflight range, Approve / Edit / Skip plan / Cancel; edit mode has a field and a drop button per sub-question and **Run**), and a **Workers** view in the run panel: one row per worker with its status, sub-question, pages read with each site's logo, pages and tokens against its slice, and a cancel button. The working line says "Researching · 2 running · 1 of 3 done".
- `Run` carries a `DeepRun` (plan, plan state, workers) rebuilt from the events, so a reopened chat shows the same workers; `StepKind::Plan` and `StepKind::Worker`.

### Added (PR #33)

- **Claim verdicts** (`tern-research`, GPUI-free and tested offline): after each answer, Tern finds its claims (every sentence with a `[n]` citation; the closing link list is read for which page each number means, never as claims) and checks each against the text Tern Crawl read for the pages it cites. Verdicts: **Supported**, **Partly supported**, **Not supported**, **No source**, each with the quoted evidence passage and an anchor to it (a `#:~:text=` fragment, or `#page=N` for a PDF page). Two paths: the chat's own model judges the best three passages per claim (one call, up to 16 claims, temperature 0), and a deterministic word-overlap check (content words, every number present, negation agreeing) gives the verdict offline and fills in for anything the model does not answer. Seeded fixtures cover supported, misquoted number, contradicted, unrelated, and unreachable sources.
- **Run reports**: each answered turn writes `runs/<chat>/turn-<n>.md` and `.json` in the data folder: the question, plan and steps, every source read with its reader (Tern Crawl), robots.txt result, and cache hit or miss, the claims with verdicts and evidence, model calls, tool calls, tokens and time against the turn's budget, and errors. Built from the event log alone (`tern_agent::report`), so any past turn can be rebuilt.
- `tern report [chat] [--turn N] [--json] [--out FILE] [--list]` prints or exports a report (`last` by default, or a chat id or its start). A turn that was never checked is checked then, by word overlap.
- Desktop: a verdict badge after each checked claim in the answer (click to open that claim in the report), a **Report** view in the run panel (tally, each claim with its verdict glyph, quoted evidence, and its source row with the site's logo; **Open report** and **JSON** open the saved files), and the working line says "Checking 3 claims" while it runs. The TUI logs the check too.
- Events `claims.checking` and `claims.checked` (verdicts, tally, report paths, judge tokens); `read_url` events carry a `crawl` block (reader, robots, cache). `Fetcher::has_cached`.
